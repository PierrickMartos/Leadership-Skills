# Email Review: Sprint 22 Retro Follow-Up

## Overall Assessment

This email has the right instinct — following up after a rough sprint is the right move. But the current draft is likely to land badly. It leads with the manager's frustration, assigns blame implicitly to the team, and closes with a directive that feels punitive. The team will read this as a telling-off, which tends to produce defensiveness rather than the behavioral change you're looking for.

---

## What's Working

- You're following up, which signals you take delivery seriously.
- You name the pattern clearly (third sprint, scope creep, estimation).
- The ask is concrete (review estimates before planning).

---

## What's Not Working

### 1. You're leading with your emotional state

> "Honestly, I'm a bit frustrated..."

Sharing frustration in a post-mortem email puts your feelings at the center of the message. The team didn't fail to manage your emotions — they had a bad sprint. Starting here signals that their performance is affecting your mood, which creates anxiety and a desire to appease rather than a desire to improve.

### 2. The framing implies the team is not trying

> "It feels like we're not learning from our mistakes"

This is an interpretation, not a fact. It may feel true to you, but the team may have very different explanations for why the pattern is repeating. Stating it as fact closes the door on diagnosis before you've opened it.

### 3. Stakeholder pressure is used as a threat

> "I'm worried about what this signals to stakeholders"

Mentioning stakeholders in this context lands as "you're making me look bad." Even if that's a real concern, it's not the right lever to pull in a retrospective follow-up. It shifts the motivation from improvement to image management.

### 4. The closing directive puts all accountability on individuals

> "Please review your story point estimates before the next sprint planning session."

Individual estimation review is part of the fix, but it implies the problem is individual carelessness. If scope creep is a systemic issue (unclear acceptance criteria, late requirements changes, pressure to commit to too much), individual review won't fix it. This also leaves no room for the team to contribute to the solution.

### 5. Tone mismatch for a retrospective context

A retro is a space for honest, blameless reflection. This email re-opens the retro in a blame-adjacent register. If you just had the retro, the team already processed this together. A follow-up that goes harder than the retro itself is demoralizing.

---

## Coaching Recommendations

1. **Separate your frustration from the message.** Acknowledge the outcome, not your emotional reaction to it.
2. **Name the pattern as a shared problem, not a team failure.** Use "we" where appropriate — you're part of the system too.
3. **Ask before telling.** If you want better behavior, invite the team to identify what's getting in the way. You'll get better answers and more buy-in.
4. **Close with a next step, not a directive.** Something that involves the team in solving the problem, not just complying with your instruction.
5. **Keep it short.** If the retro already covered this, the email should be a brief, forward-looking summary — not a second retrospective.

---

## Suggested Rewrite

---

**Subject: Sprint 22 — follow-up and what's next**

Hi team,

Thanks for the honest conversation in today's retro. Three sprints with the same pattern — scope creep and estimation drift — tells me something structural needs to change, not just more effort.

Before our next planning session, I'd like us to spend 15 minutes together looking at what's making estimation hard. Is it late requirements? Pressure to commit before things are fully defined? Something else? I want to understand the root cause before we land on a fix.

In the meantime, if you have thoughts on what's driving this, I'd welcome them — reply here or flag me directly.

Thanks

---

## Why the Rewrite Works Better

- **No emotional framing** — the problem is named without making it about how the manager feels.
- **Pattern is acknowledged as systemic** — "something structural needs to change" invites diagnosis rather than assigning blame.
- **Concrete next step is collaborative** — 15 minutes to understand root cause, not a unilateral directive.
- **Invites input** — gives the team a way to participate in the solution before the meeting.
- **Shorter** — respects that the retro already happened; this is a bridge to the next step, not a reprimand.
