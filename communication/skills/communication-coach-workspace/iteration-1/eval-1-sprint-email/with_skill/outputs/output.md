# Communication Coach — Sprint 22 Retro Email Review

**Medium:** Email
**Audience:** Direct reports (the team)
**Purpose:** Follow up after a sprint that didn't hit its goal; prompt the team to improve estimation before next planning

---

## Original

> **Subject: Sprint 22 retro**
>
> Hi team,
>
> I wanted to take a moment to follow up on our sprint 22 retrospective earlier today. As you know, we didn't hit our sprint goal this week. Honestly, I'm a bit frustrated because this is the third sprint in a row where we've had issues with scope creep and estimation. It feels like we're not learning from our mistakes and I'm worried about what this signals to stakeholders. I think everyone needs to think more carefully before committing to work they can't complete.
>
> We need to do better. Please review your story point estimates before the next sprint planning session.
>
> Thanks

---

## Revised

> **Subject: Sprint 22 — what we're changing before next planning**
>
> Hi team,
>
> For the next sprint planning session, please review your story point estimates before committing — and flag scope changes as they surface rather than at the end of the sprint.
>
> This is the third sprint in a row where scope creep and estimation gaps caused us to miss the sprint goal. That pattern is costing us credibility with stakeholders and it's something we can fix together. I'll add 15 minutes to the pre-planning agenda so we can calibrate as a group before we commit.
>
> If you want to talk through what got in the way this sprint, my door is open before Monday.
>
> Thanks

---

## What changed

- **BLUF (buried lead):** The original opens with "I wanted to take a moment to follow up..." — seven words before anything useful. The revised version leads with the concrete ask in the first sentence. Readers know immediately what they need to do.

- **Facts, not feelings:** Three phrases in the original are feelings presented as facts: "I'm a bit frustrated," "it feels like we're not learning," and "I'm worried about what this signals." These shift the reader's attention to managing the sender's emotional state rather than solving the problem. The revised version replaces them with the observable fact (third consecutive missed sprint goal) and its consequence (stakeholder credibility). One sentence. More credible.

- **Positive framing / deficit close:** "We need to do better" and "work they can't complete" are deficit framings that land as blame without giving the team a path forward. The revised version acknowledges the pattern plainly, then immediately pivots to what's being done about it (15 minutes added to pre-planning). The close offers support rather than a warning.

- **Subject line:** "Sprint 22 retro" describes the event that already happened. "Sprint 22 — what we're changing before next planning" tells the reader why this email matters and what it's asking for — even in the inbox preview.
