# Feedback on Skip-Level Delay Message (Without Skill)

## Input Message

**Subject:** FYI on Project Atlas

Hi Sarah,

I wanted to loop you in on a situation with Project Atlas. We've been running into some challenges lately. The integration with the payments API has been more complex than we initially anticipated and we've had some resource constraints due to the recent re-org. As a result, we're going to need to push the go-live date from March 15 to April 3rd. I know this isn't ideal and I'm sorry for the disruption this might cause. We're doing everything we can to minimize the impact.

Please let me know if you have any questions.

Best,
Alex

---

## Feedback

**Overall Assessment**

The message is polite and communicates the basic facts, but it underdelivers for a skip-level audience. It reads as a courtesy loop-in rather than a confident leadership update. A skip-level expects situational clarity, a mitigation plan, and confidence in the team — not just an apology.

---

**What Works**

- The subject line is clear and the message is concise.
- You proactively flagged the issue rather than waiting until the last moment.
- You named the two root causes (API complexity, re-org resourcing).

---

**What Needs Improvement**

**1. No mitigation plan or recovery path**

The message ends with "we're doing everything we can" — which is vague and signals that you may not have a concrete plan. A skip-level will immediately ask: what exactly are you doing? What's the recovery plan? What's the risk of further slippage? You should answer these before they have to ask.

**2. Over-apologizing undermines confidence**

"I know this isn't ideal and I'm sorry for the disruption this might cause" is soft language that positions you as reactive rather than in control. One brief acknowledgment is enough — then pivot to the plan.

**3. The new date lands without context**

April 3rd is dropped without explanation of how you got there. Is this a firm commitment or a revised estimate? Is there buffer built in? Skip-levels care about whether the new date is reliable, not just what it is.

**4. No ask or clear next step**

"Please let me know if you have any questions" is passive. Does Sarah need to take any action? Does anyone above her need to be notified? Are there downstream teams or commitments that need to be adjusted? If there is nothing she needs to do, say so explicitly — "no action needed on your end."

**5. Missing: impact awareness**

You mention "disruption" generically but don't name the downstream impact. Does this affect other teams, a customer commitment, a product launch, a board date? Sarah needs to know whether this is a contained delay or a cascading one.

---

**Suggested Rewrite**

> Subject: Project Atlas — Go-Live Pushed to April 3rd
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> Wanted to flag a schedule change on Project Atlas before you hear it elsewhere.
>
> We're moving the go-live from March 15 to April 3rd. Two factors drove this: the payments API integration turned out to be significantly more complex than scoped, and we lost two weeks of velocity from the re-org. April 3rd is a firm date with buffer built in — we're not expecting further slippage.
>
> What we're doing to close the gap: [specific actions — e.g., we've brought in a second engineer on the API work, we've descoped X feature to post-launch, we're running daily syncs with the payments team].
>
> Downstream impact: [e.g., this does not affect the Q2 customer commitment / this does push the launch announcement by three weeks — I've already aligned with Marketing].
>
> No action needed from you. I'll keep [your manager] updated and flag immediately if the April 3rd date is at risk.
>
> Alex

---

**Priority Fixes (if you keep the shorter format)**

1. Replace "we're doing everything we can" with one or two concrete actions.
2. State whether April 3rd is firm and why.
3. Name the downstream impact, even if it's "contained to this team."
4. Replace the passive close with an explicit "no action needed" or a specific ask.
