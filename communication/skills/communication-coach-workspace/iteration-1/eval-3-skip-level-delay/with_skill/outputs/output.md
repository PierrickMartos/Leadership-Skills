# Communication Coach — Feedback on Project Atlas Delay Email

**Medium**: Email
**Audience**: Skip-level (Sarah)
**Purpose**: Inform of project delay; shift go-live date from March 15 to April 3

---

## Side-by-Side Review

**Original**

> Subject: FYI on Project Atlas
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> I wanted to loop you in on a situation with Project Atlas. We've been running into some challenges lately. The integration with the payments API has been more complex than we initially anticipated and we've had some resource constraints due to the recent re-org. As a result, we're going to need to push the go-live date from March 15 to April 3rd. I know this isn't ideal and I'm sorry for the disruption this might cause. We're doing everything we can to minimize the impact.
>
> Please let me know if you have any questions.
>
> Best,
> Alex

---

**Revised**

> Subject: Project Atlas go-live moving to April 3 — here's why and what we're doing about it
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> Project Atlas go-live is moving from March 15 to April 3. I wanted you to have this directly from me before it shows up in any reporting.
>
> Two factors drove the shift: the payments API integration required 3 additional weeks of work beyond our original scope estimate, and two engineers moved to other teams during the re-org. The new date gives us a realistic path to a clean launch rather than a rushed one.
>
> Here's where things stand:
> - Integration work is 80% complete; we expect to close it out by March 22.
> - QA and UAT are scheduled for March 23–31.
> - April 3 is a firm target with no known blockers at this point.
>
> No action needed from you — I'll send a brief update if anything changes before launch. Happy to talk through it if useful.
>
> Best,
> Alex

---

**What changed**

- **BLUF — Subject line**: "FYI on Project Atlas" gives Sarah no information. The revised subject states the decision, the date, and signals that context follows — so she can act on it before even opening the email.

- **BLUF — Lead sentence**: The original opens with "I wanted to loop you in on a situation" — a five-word preamble before anything useful. The revised version puts the key fact (the new date) in the first sentence. Sarah should never have to reach the third sentence to find out why she's being emailed.

- **Facts, not feelings**: "I know this isn't ideal and I'm sorry for the disruption" shifts the tone toward managing Alex's anxiety rather than informing Sarah. It's not that apologies are wrong — it's that they shouldn't substitute for facts. The revision removes the apology and replaces it with specific data: what caused the delay (payments API scope, two engineers re-orged), how far along the work is, and what the remaining schedule looks like. Sarah can now assess the situation herself rather than be asked to absorb Alex's feelings about it.

- **Positive framing**: "We've been running into some challenges" and "we're going to need to push" frame the message around what went wrong. The revision reframes around the path to a clean launch — the April 3 date is presented as a deliberate, informed choice, not a failure being disclosed. This is not spin; the facts are the same. The frame is different.

- **Concise and direct — closing**: "We're doing everything we can to minimize the impact" is vague reassurance. It adds length without adding information. The revision replaces it with a specific close: what Sarah can expect next, and a clear signal that no action is needed from her (which is actually the most useful thing a skip-level often needs to know).
